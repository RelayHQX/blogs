Meet Rover: The Intelligence Behind Relay Mail

Email contains more than messages.

Behind every email, there could be an order waiting to arrive, a payment that needs to be recorded, an appointment coming up, or a verification code needed to sign in somewhere.

Yet most email applications treat all of this information in almost the same way. They show you a message, and leave you to find the important details yourself.

Email services like Gmail have made organizing messages easier through categories, labels, search, and filters. But we believe there is another problem worth solving.

What if your email application could understand the information inside your emails instead of simply displaying them?

Imagine receiving a delivery notification and immediately seeing its tracking number and expected delivery date. Or receiving a verification code that Relay can identify and make accessible without searching through the entire message.

The information is already there. The challenge is understanding it.

That is why we are building Rover, the intelligent email-processing engine behind Relay Mail.

«"Gmail gives Relay the emails. Rover gives Relay an understanding of those emails."»

---

The Problem: Emails Are More Than Messages

Think about your inbox for a moment.

You might receive emails from your bank, shopping websites, colleagues, social networks, applications, and people you know.

Although they arrive in the same inbox, they serve completely different purposes.

Consider these examples:

Email| What you actually need
Delivery notification| Tracking number and delivery date
Payment confirmation| Amount, merchant, and transaction ID
Login verification| The verification code
Flight confirmation| Flight number and boarding time
Meeting invitation| Date, time, and meeting details
Invoice| Amount, invoice number, and due date

You usually have to open each message, read through its contents, and find the information you need.

Sometimes, several emails are even related to the same activity. An order confirmation, payment receipt, shipping notification, and delivery update might all belong to one purchase.

But they remain separate messages in your inbox.

This creates unnecessary work.

- <Search className="size-4.5 inline text-purple-500 mr-2" /> **Finding information:** Important details are buried inside messages.
- <Layers className="size-4.5 inline text-blue-500 mr-2" /> Connecting information: Related emails remain separated.
- <Clock className="size-4.5 inline text-amber-500 mr-2" /> Saving time: Small tasks require repeatedly opening and reading emails.
- <Inbox className="size-4.5 inline text-emerald-500 mr-2" /> Organizing messages: Traditional categories don't always explain what an email actually contains.

We don't believe users should have to do all this work manually.

---

Introducing Rover

Rover is designed to work behind the scenes inside Relay Mail.

Its responsibility is to understand incoming emails, extract useful information, classify messages, and help Relay decide how that information should be presented.

Rover is built around two main systems:

<Layers className="size-5 inline text-blue-500 mr-2" /> Rover Extractor

Reads email content and finds useful information, such as verification codes, order numbers, payment amounts, dates, and tracking references.

<Sliders className="size-5 inline text-emerald-500 mr-2" /> Rover Filter

Uses extracted information, sender details, and user-defined rules to categorize, prioritize, and organize emails.

They have different responsibilities, but share one purpose: making email easier to understand and manage.

---

Rover Extractor: Finding What Matters

The Extractor is the first major part of Rover.

It reads the available information in an email, including its sender, subject, body, headers, and supported attachment details.

It then looks for recognizable information and converts it into structured data that Relay can use.

Think of it as having someone read an email and write down only the details that matter.

What can Rover Extract?

Rover is designed to recognize several types of information.

Authentication and verification

<KeyRound className="size-4.5 inline text-emerald-500 mr-2" /> Verification codes, OTPs, two-factor authentication codes, magic links, and password reset links.

Shopping and deliveries

<Package className="size-4.5 inline text-blue-500 mr-2" /> Order numbers, tracking IDs, courier names, delivery dates, and shipment status.

Financial information

<CreditCard className="size-4.5 inline text-purple-500 mr-2" /> Transaction amounts, invoices, payment confirmations, refunds, and payment due dates.

Travel information

<Plane className="size-4.5 inline text-sky-500 mr-2" /> Flight numbers, boarding times, booking references, hotel reservations, and check-in dates.

Events and schedules

<Calendar className="size-4.5 inline text-amber-500 mr-2" /> Meeting dates, event times, appointments, invitations, and deadlines.

People and contacts

<MessageSquare className="size-4.5 inline text-pink-500 mr-2" /> Names, email addresses, phone numbers, organizations, and contact details.

Documents and attachments

<FileText className="size-4.5 inline text-neutral-500 mr-2" /> Invoice numbers, document references, attachment names, and document types.

From an email to useful information

Let's take a real-world example.

Imagine receiving this email:

«Your Amazon order #402918 has been shipped. Expected delivery: November 14. Track your package using tracking ID XYZ123456.»

A normal email application displays the entire message.

Rover's Extractor would look for the useful details and produce a structured result.

Example extraction result:

{
  "type": "delivery",
  "entities": {
    "order_id": "402918",
    "tracking_id": "XYZ123456",
    "expected_delivery": "2026-11-14",
    "merchant": "Amazon"
  }
}

This is what makes extraction useful.

Instead of keeping everything as plain text, Rover turns the information into something Relay can understand and work with.

For example, Relay could use the extracted information to display:

Delivery information

Field| Value
Merchant| Amazon
Order| #402918
Tracking ID| XYZ123456
Expected delivery| November 14

The original email is still available. Rover simply makes its important information easier to access.

Rover Filter: Knowing Where Emails Belong

Extracting information is only half the job.

After understanding what an email contains, Rover needs to help Relay decide how to organize it.

This is the responsibility of the Filter.

Traditional email categories are useful. Primary, Social, Promotions, Updates, and Forums help separate different types of messages.

We want to build on that foundation rather than replace it unnecessarily.

General email categories

Category| Purpose
Primary| Important personal and work conversations
Social| Social network notifications
Promotions| Offers, discounts, and marketing
Updates| Receipts, shipping, and service notifications
Forums| Mailing lists and group discussions
Spam| Suspicious and unwanted messages

But an email's category doesn't always explain its actual purpose.

A delivery notification and a payment receipt might both appear under Updates, even though users may want to access them differently.

That's why Rover can also recognize purpose-based categories.

Purpose-based organization

Purpose| What Rover identifies
Authentication| OTPs and verification links
Deliveries| Orders and package tracking
Transactions| Payments, invoices, and refunds
Upcoming| Meetings and deadlines
Travel| Flights and reservations
Documents| Important files and attachments

An email can have a general category and a specific purpose at the same time.

For example:

{
  "category": "updates",
  "purpose": "delivery",
  "priority": "normal"
}

This gives Relay more information than a simple folder or label.

Priority and custom rules

Not every email deserves the same level of attention.

A message from a colleague about an important deadline may need more attention than an automated promotional notification.

Rover can use different signals to help determine priority:

- Sender and contact information.
- Message content and subject.
- Extracted dates and deadlines.
- User preferences.
- Custom filtering rules.

Users should also be able to create their own rules.

For example:

IF sender is from my workplace
AND email contains a meeting invitation

THEN:
  category = Primary
  purpose = Upcoming
  priority = Important

This is an example of the kind of flexible organization we want Rover to support.

The objective is not to hide emails automatically or make decisions users cannot control. It is to make organization more useful and predictable.

How Rover Works Internally

Rover is designed as a processing pipeline.

An incoming email passes through several stages before its results become available to Relay.

Here is the proposed architecture:

          INCOMING EMAIL
                 |
                 v
          +--------------+
          |    Parser    |
          +--------------+
                 |
                 v
          +--------------+
          |  Extractor   |
          +--------------+
                 |
                 v
          +--------------+
          |  Classifier  |
          +--------------+
                 |
                 v
          +--------------+
          | Filter Engine|
          +--------------+
                 |
                 v
       +--------------------+
       | Structured Results |
       +--------------------+
                 |
                 v
          +--------------+
          |  Relay Mail  |
          +--------------+
                 |
                 v
       Smart Inbox and UI

Let's understand what happens at each stage.

Parser

The Parser prepares an email so the rest of Rover can work with it.

Emails are not always simple text. They can contain HTML, attachments, quoted replies, formatting, and other information.

The Parser handles this structure and prepares readable content.

Extractor

The Extractor searches the prepared content for useful details.

It uses patterns and specialized detection rules to identify things such as OTPs, order numbers, dates, and amounts.

Classifier

The Classifier determines what an email is about.

It considers the sender, subject, message content, and extracted information instead of relying on a single keyword.

Filter Engine

The Filter Engine applies categories, priorities, custom rules, and user preferences.

It decides how the email should be organized.

Structured Results

Finally, Rover produces structured results that Relay can use to improve the inbox and display useful information.

The processing engine and the user interface remain separate. Rover understands the email; Relay decides how to present that understanding.

Building Rover Without Depending on AI APIs

We don't believe every email needs to be sent to a large AI model to become useful.

Many common email patterns can be recognized using traditional programming techniques.

For example, verification codes, tracking numbers, dates, and transaction amounts often follow recognizable patterns.

We want Rover to use these patterns to perform common tasks locally, without requiring an external AI service for every email.

Here are some of the technologies and techniques involved:

Technology| Responsibility
Regular expressions| Finding OTPs, IDs, dates, and amounts
Sender registry| Recognizing known companies and domains
Pattern recognition| Identifying common email structures
MIME parser| Reading email content and attachments
HTML normalizer| Preparing readable email content
Rule engine| Applying categories and user preferences
Confidence scoring| Measuring how reliable a detection is

Why confidence matters

Consider two emails.

The first is sent from a recognized delivery service and contains an order number, tracking ID, and expected delivery date.

The second simply mentions the word "delivery" in a conversation.

A basic keyword filter might treat both messages the same way.

Rover should do better.

It should combine multiple signals before deciding what an email represents.

For example:

{
  "type": "delivery",
  "confidence": 0.96,
  "entities": {
    "order_id": "402918",
    "tracking_id": "XYZ123456"
  }
}

The confidence value is an illustrative example, not a measured Rover benchmark.

When information is uncertain, Relay should avoid presenting it as confirmed fact.

Our goal is useful understanding, not unnecessary guesses.

Rover Intelligence Index: Connecting Information

This is where Rover becomes more interesting than a traditional email filter.

Today, email applications generally treat messages as individual items, even when several messages are related to the same activity.

But people don't think in individual emails.

They think about their orders, payments, appointments, reservations, and conversations.

We want Relay to understand those connections.

One order, multiple emails

Imagine purchasing something online.

You receive:

- An order confirmation.
- A payment confirmation.
- A shipping notification.
- A delivery update.

These are four separate emails, but they all belong to the same purchase.

Rover could identify common details, such as the order number and merchant, and connect the messages.

Relay could then present them as one delivery timeline.

        YOUR ORDER #402918
                |
                v
       Order Confirmed
                |
                v
       Payment Received
                |
                v
       Package Shipped
                |
                v
       Out for Delivery
                |
                v
        Package Delivered

Instead of searching through several messages, users could see the progress of their order in one place.

Other possibilities

Information| What Relay could provide
Orders| A connected delivery timeline
Payments| A transaction history
Appointments| An upcoming schedule
Travel| A connected travel record
Verification| Quickly accessible authentication codes

We call this direction the Rover Intelligence Index.

It is about connecting information that belongs together instead of simply collecting more emails.

This is an important distinction: Rover should not invent relationships. It must use matching information and reliable signals before connecting messages.

What We're Building First

Rover is a substantial system, and we don't want to build everything at once.

We want to start with the most useful capabilities and expand from there.

Our development priorities include:

Component| Responsibility| Priority
MIME and HTML Parser| Prepare email content| Foundation
Authentication Extractor| Identify OTPs and verification links| High
Sender Registry| Recognize known senders| High
Category Classifier| Organize common email types| High
Transaction Extractor| Identify payments and invoices| High
Delivery Extractor| Identify orders and tracking information| High
Event Extractor| Identify dates and appointments| Medium
Custom Filter Engine| Support user-defined rules| Medium
Confidence Engine| Validate extraction results| High
Intelligence Index| Connect related emails| Later

We intend to develop Rover as an independent, reusable TypeScript processing library within Relay Mail.

Keeping it independent gives us room to improve extraction, classification, and filtering without tightly coupling those systems to the email interface.

It also means the same processing engine can support future Relay features.

Designed With Privacy in Mind

Email can contain extremely personal information.

It may include financial records, private conversations, verification codes, travel details, and other sensitive information.

That makes privacy an important part of Rover's design.

Our initial direction is to process information directly on the device wherever possible.

We want to avoid unnecessary external services, reduce dependencies, and keep the processing lightweight.

The goal is simple:

- <Lock className="size-4.5 inline text-emerald-500 mr-2" /> Local processing: Handle common extraction and classification tasks on the device.
- <ShieldCheck className="size-4.5 inline text-blue-500 mr-2" /> Controlled access: Only process the email information needed for a particular task.
- <EyeOff className="size-4.5 inline text-purple-500 mr-2" /> Less unnecessary sharing: Avoid sending message contents to external AI services for routine processing.
- <Sliders className="size-4.5 inline text-amber-500 mr-2" /> User control: Let users influence how messages are organized.

Local processing is a design goal, not a claim that every part of Rover is already implemented or that the entire Gmail connection operates offline.

You shouldn't have to open a special Rover screen just to find your verification code or understand a delivery notification.
