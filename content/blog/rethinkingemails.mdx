Email contains far more than messages.

Behind every email in your queue, there could be a parcel waiting to arrive, a payment that needs balancing, an upcoming calendar commitment, or a one-time passcode needed to access an account.

Yet traditional email applications treat all of this data as generic text. They render raw HTML and force you to dig through corporate footers, disclaimers, and nested reply chains just to locate the two details you actually care about.

Email providers have introduced basic categorization with tabs, labels, search queries, and rule filters. But that only organizes the container — it doesn't solve the core problem.

What if your email client could understand the structured data inside your inbox rather than just displaying raw text?

Imagine opening a shipping notification and seeing the courier tracking number, real-time status, and expected delivery window instantly pinned at the top. Or receiving an authentication email and having the one-time code extracted and ready to copy with a single tap.

The information is already there. The challenge is extracting and structuring it.

That is why we built **Rover**: the intelligent, client-side email processing engine powering Relay Mail.

> **"Gmail gives Relay the emails. Rover gives Relay an understanding of those emails."**

## The Problem: Emails Are More Than Messages

Think about the sheer variety of traffic arriving in your inbox daily:

- Bank alerts and wire receipts
- E-commerce order and shipping notifications
- Flight tickets and hotel bookings
- Two-factor authentication and magic links
- Calendar invites and reschedule notices
- Critical personal and work correspondence

Although these messages land in the exact same list view, their underlying data structures and utilities are completely distinct.

| Incoming Email Type | What You Actually Need |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Delivery notification** | Tracking number, carrier, and estimated arrival date |
| **Payment confirmation** | Total amount, currency, merchant name, and transaction ID |

| **Meeting invitation** | Date, local time zone, agenda, and video call link |
| **Invoice & Bill** | Due date, total amount owed, and direct payment link |

Today, you are forced to open each message, squint past legal disclaimers, and manually extract those key facts.

Worse, multiple emails frequently belong to a single overarching real-world event. An order confirmation, payment receipt, dispatch alert, and delivery confirmation might arrive across four days as separate threads — cluttering your inbox as disconnected fragments.

This introduces unnecessary cognitive overhead:

## Introducing Rover

Rover is the intelligence runtime operating quietly beneath Relay Mail.

Its job is to parse incoming message payloads, isolate high-value entities, categorize context, and inform Relay's UI on how best to render the data cleanly.

Rover consists of two core cooperating systems:

- <Layers className="size-5 inline text-blue-500 mr-2" /> **Rover Extractor:** Evaluates MIME bodies to extract entity primitives — including OTPs, order identifiers, monetary amounts, timestamps, and courier numbers.
- <Sliders className="size-5 inline text-emerald-500 mr-2" /> **Rover Filter:** Combines extracted primitives, sender reputation graphs, and custom heuristic rules to categorize, rank, and organize your inbox.

Together, they transform unstructured text into structured, actionable data.

## Rover Extractor: Finding What Matters

The Extractor parses the raw email headers, MIME parts, clean plain-text bodies, sanitized HTML nodes, and verified attachments.

It transforms messy unstructured text into strict, validated JSON schemas.

### What Rover Extracts

Rover is trained to detect structured entities across common transactional domains:

- <KeyRound className="size-4.5 inline text-emerald-500 mr-2" /> **Authentication & Security:** One-time passwords (OTPs), 2FA codes, authentication links, and password recovery triggers.
- <Package className="size-4.5 inline text-blue-500 mr-2" /> **Orders & Logistics:** Order reference numbers, live tracking numbers, courier services, dispatch statuses, and estimated delivery dates.
- <CreditCard className="size-4.5 inline text-purple-500 mr-2" /> **Billing & Finance:** Transaction totals, currency codes, invoice identifiers, VAT breakdowns, payment confirmations, and statement balances.
- <Plane className="size-4.5 inline text-sky-500 mr-2" /> **Travel & Hospitality:** Airline codes, PNR booking references, departure gates, seat allocations, and hotel check-in windows.
- <Calendar className="size-4.5 inline text-amber-500 mr-2" /> **Schedules & Events:** ICS payloads, webinar URLs, calendar invite times, agenda items, and RSVP states.
- <MessageSquare className="size-4.5 inline text-pink-500 mr-2" /> **Identity & People:** Senders, display aliases, phone numbers, domain origins, and job titles.
- <FileText className="size-4.5 inline text-neutral-500 mr-2" /> **Documents & Attachments:** PDFs, receipts, statement attachments, and contract files.

A traditional client shows this paragraph inside a cluttered HTML template with banners and upsell links. Rover extracts the signal:

The complete original email remains intact and accessible. Rover simply makes the critical information instant to read and act on.
Rover Filter: Contextual Organization
Extracting information is only half the battle. Rover also decides where messages belong and how urgently they need your attention.
Standard email categories are a solid foundation, but they are too broad:
| Default Category | Standard Intended Purpose |
|---|---|
| Primary | High-signal personal and work conversations |
| Social | Social network notifications and updates |
| Promotions | Marketing campaigns, deals, and discount offers |
| Updates | System receipts, alerts, and operational confirmations |
| Forums | Discussion boards, user groups, and distribution lists |
| Spam | Unwanted, suspicious, or spoofed messages |
A parcel delivery update and a bank tax invoice might both end up under Updates, even though one requires immediate attention and the other is for your accounting records.
Rover solves this by assigning dynamic, multi-dimensional Purposes:
| Rover Purpose | What It Represents |
|---|---|
| Authentication | High-priority security codes and login verifications |
| Deliveries | Active physical package shipments and order tracking |
| Transactions | Bank receipts, payment approvals, and invoices |
| Upcoming | Calendar invites, meetings, flights, and deadlines |
| Documents | Important signed records, PDFs, and legal assets |
Every email receives both a base category and a purpose tag:
{
  "category": "updates",
  "purpose": "delivery",
  "priority": "normal",
  "requires_action": false
}

Deterministic Priority & User Rules
Priority is calculated dynamically using transparent signals:
 * Verified contact and domain reputation
 * Time-sensitive dates, deadlines, and flight departures
 * Extracted OTP and 2FA tokens
 * Explicit user overrides and custom rule definitions
Users can construct predictable, transparent routing logic:
// Example custom user rule
if (message.sender.domain === "company.com" && message.hasPurpose("upcoming")) {
  message.setPriority("high");
  message.pinToTop();
}

No opaque algorithms deciding what you see without your consent. You remain in full control.
How Rover Works Internally
Rover operates as a sequential processing pipeline designed for speed and determinism:
          INCOMING RAW EMAIL
                 │
                 ▼
          ┌──────────────┐
          │  MIME Parser │  ── Sanitize HTML & extract plain text
          └──────────────┘
                 │
                 ▼
          ┌──────────────┐
          │   Extractor  │  ── Regex, token scanners & entity extractors
          └──────────────┘
                 │
                 ▼
          ┌──────────────┐
          │  Classifier  │  ── Purpose & intent classification
          └──────────────┘
                 │
                 ▼
          ┌──────────────┐
          │ Filter Engine│  ── Priority calculation & user heuristic rules
          └──────────────┘
                 │
                 ▼
       ┌────────────────────┐
       │  Structured Result │  ── Validated type-safe JSON entity
       └────────────────────┘
                 │
                 ▼
          ┌──────────────┐
          │  Relay UI    │  ── Instant native card & optimistic rendering
          └──────────────┘

1. MIME Parser
Normalizes messy multi-part emails, strips tracking beacons, parses inline CID attachments, and isolates the semantic body text.
2. Extractor
Scans the normalized tokens using specialized deterministic extractors (OTP pattern matchers, date-time parsers, ISO currency recognizers, and carrier tracking checksums).
3. Classifier
Evaluates the sender domain, subject structure, and extracted entity graph to assign primary and secondary purpose tags.
4. Filter Engine
Applies user-defined filters, priority sorting, and smart labeling.
5. Structured Data Store
Outputs lightweight, queryable records ready for Relay’s React Native and web views to render without blocking the main UI thread.
Local-First Architecture: No External AI Dependency
We don't believe your inbox needs to be streamed to third-party LLM endpoints to parse an OTP or delivery date.
Sending sensitive emails to third-party cloud APIs introduces latency, costs, and significant privacy risks. Most email structures follow deterministic patterns that can be solved with robust, battle-tested software engineering.
| Engineering Approach | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Deterministic Regular Expressions | Extracting verification tokens, tracking numbers, and currency figures |
| Sender Domain Registry | Authenticating senders via SPF/DKIM verification |
| Micro-Parsers | Processing structured microdata (schema.org, JSON-LD, calendar ICS) |
| MIME Normalizer | Cleaning raw multi-part payloads and stripping telemetry pixels |
| Rule Heuristics | Calculating priority levels and handling custom sorting rules |
| Confidence Scoring Engine | Validating that extracted dates and tracking numbers pass validation rules |
Why Confidence Matters
If a friend sends an email mentioning "I'm waiting on my Amazon delivery," a naive keyword scanner might incorrectly mark that thread as a shipping notification.
Rover calculates multi-signal confidence scores before generating an entity card:
{
  "type": "delivery",
  "confidence": 0.99,
  "factors": {
    "authenticated_sender": true,
    "valid_tracking_checksum": true,
    "has_order_format": true
  }
}

If the confidence score falls below a verified threshold, Relay suppresses the rich card and falls back cleanly to the standard message view. No hallucinations, no false assumptions.
