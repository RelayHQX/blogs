Email has become an essential part of our everyday lives. We receive work messages, shopping updates, payment confirmations, meeting invitations, verification codes, and countless other notifications through it.

Email services like Gmail have made it easier to manage these messages. We can search, organize, label, archive, and filter thousands of emails.

But there is still something missing.

Your email application can show you a message. But can it understand what that message actually means?

When a delivery confirmation arrives, you don't really want to read an entire email just to find out when your package will arrive. When a payment receipt arrives, you don't want to search through paragraphs to find the amount. And when a verification code arrives, you don't want to open an entire conversation just to copy six digits.

The information is already there. It is simply buried inside messages.

We believe email should work harder to bring that information to you.

That is why we are building Rover.

«"Email shouldn't just deliver information. It should help you understand it."»

The Problem With How We Handle Email

Think about how many different things arrive in your inbox every day.

A message from your bank, a delivery update from a shopping website, an invitation from a colleague, and a verification code from an application.

They are all emails, but they don't all serve the same purpose.

Traditional email organization mainly focuses on deciding where a message belongs. Primary, Promotions, Social, Updates, and other categories help keep things organized.

These are useful features. But organizing messages is only part of the problem.

Consider a simple example:

You receive an email saying:

«Your order #402918 has been shipped. Expected delivery: November 14. Your tracking ID is XYZ123456.»

You open it, read through the message, find the tracking number, remember the delivery date, and close it.

Now imagine receiving three more updates about the same order.

You have four emails, but what you really care about is one thing: Where is my package?

The same problem happens with payments, appointments, reservations, and verification codes.

- <Mail className="size-4.5 inline text-blue-500 mr-2" /> Messages contain information: Important details are hidden inside long emails.
- <Search className="size-4.5 inline text-purple-500 mr-2" /> **Finding information takes effort:** You often have to open and search through messages.
- <Layers className="size-4.5 inline text-emerald-500 mr-2" /> Related emails stay separate: Multiple updates about the same thing appear as individual messages.
- <Clock className="size-4.5 inline text-amber-500 mr-2" /> Small tasks consume time: Finding one date, amount, or code can take longer than it should.

We don't think people should have to do all this work themselves.

Introducing Rover

Rover is the intelligent email-processing engine behind Relay Mail.

Its job is simple: understand what an email contains, find the information that matters, and help Relay decide how to present it.

Instead of treating every email as just another block of text, Rover looks for the useful details inside it.

It can identify things like:

- <KeyRound className="size-4.5 inline text-emerald-500 mr-2" /> Verification codes: OTPs, security codes, and verification links.
- <Package className="size-4.5 inline text-blue-500 mr-2" /> Orders and deliveries: Order numbers, tracking details, and expected delivery dates.
- <Wallet className="size-4.5 inline text-purple-500 mr-2" /> Payments and receipts: Transaction amounts, payment confirmations, invoices, and refunds.
- <Plane className="size-4.5 inline text-sky-500 mr-2" /> Travel information: Flight details, booking references, and hotel reservations.
- <CalendarDays className="size-4.5 inline text-amber-500 mr-2" /> Events and appointments: Meeting invitations, schedules, and important deadlines.
- <Users className="size-4.5 inline text-pink-500 mr-2" /> People and organizations: Contact details, names, and sender information.
- <FileText className="size-4.5 inline text-neutral-500 mr-2" /> Documents: Invoices, attachments, and document references.

The goal isn't to make email more complicated with artificial intelligence features.

It is to remove the unnecessary work between receiving information and understanding it.

Two Parts, One Purpose

Rover is built around two main components: the Extractor and the Filter.

They have different responsibilities, but they work together.

The Extractor: Finding What Matters

<ScanSearch className="size-5 inline text-emerald-500 mr-2" /> The Extractor looks inside an email and finds useful information.

Imagine receiving a delivery notification.

Instead of leaving everything buried in the original message, Rover can identify the important details:

The original email:

«Your order #402918 has been shipped. Expected delivery: November 14. Track your package using tracking ID XYZ123456.»

What Rover understands:

Information| Extracted detail
Type| Delivery
Order number| #402918
Tracking ID| XYZ123456
Expected delivery| November 14
Merchant| Amazon

Relay can then use this information to show a cleaner delivery summary.

The same approach applies to payment confirmations, verification codes, travel bookings, and other types of email.

You don't need to read everything to find the one thing you need.

The Filter: Knowing Where Things Belong

<Filter className="size-5 inline text-blue-500 mr-2" /> Finding information is only half the job.

Rover also needs to understand what kind of message it is and how it should be organized.

The Filter uses information from the Extractor, along with the sender and other message details, to make better decisions.

For example:

Category| What belongs there
Primary| Personal and important conversations
Social| Social network notifications
Promotions| Offers and marketing messages
Updates| Receipts, shipping, and service notifications
Forums| Mailing lists and group discussions
Spam| Unwanted or suspicious messages

But we want to go beyond these familiar categories.

Rover can also recognize specific purposes:

- <KeyRound className="size-4 inline text-emerald-500 mr-2" /> Authentication: Verification codes and login links.
- <Truck className="size-4 inline text-blue-500 mr-2" /> Deliveries: Orders and package updates.
- <Receipt className="size-4 inline text-purple-500 mr-2" /> Transactions: Payments, invoices, and refunds.
- <CalendarClock className="size-4 inline text-amber-500 mr-2" /> Upcoming: Meetings, events, and deadlines.

This means an email can belong to a familiar inbox category while also being recognized for its actual purpose.

Organization should help you find information, not force you to understand a complicated folder system.

How Rover Works

We don't want Rover to depend on expensive AI services or require every email to be sent to an external service for analysis.

Our approach is to build a lightweight processing engine that can understand common email patterns directly on your device.

Here is how the process works:

<Mail className="size-5 inline text-blue-500 mr-2" /> Receive the email

Relay receives the original message through your connected email account.

<FileText className="size-5 inline text-purple-500 mr-2" /> Read its contents

Rover prepares the message by understanding its structure, removing unnecessary formatting, and making its content easier to process.

<ScanSearch className="size-5 inline text-emerald-500 mr-2" /> Find useful details

It looks for recognizable information such as verification codes, order numbers, dates, amounts, and tracking references.

<Brain className="size-5 inline text-amber-500 mr-2" /> Understand the message

Rover combines the sender, subject, message content, and extracted details to determine what the email is about.

<Filter className="size-5 inline text-indigo-500 mr-2" /> Organize it

The Filter applies categories, user preferences, and rules to decide how the message should be organized.

<Sparkles className="size-5 inline text-emerald-500 mr-2" /> Present what matters

Relay uses the results to create clearer message views, useful summaries, and relevant information cards.

The entire process is designed to happen without making the user do the work manually.

Not Every Email Needs Artificial Intelligence

There is a common assumption that making software intelligent means sending everything to a large AI model.

We don't believe that is always necessary.

Many things inside emails follow recognizable patterns.

A verification code is usually a short sequence of numbers. An invoice contains recognizable amounts and references. A delivery notification often includes an order number and a tracking ID.

These details can be found using carefully designed rules and pattern recognition.

That is the direction we are taking with Rover.

- <Code className="size-4.5 inline text-blue-500 mr-2" /> Pattern recognition: Find codes, dates, amounts, and reference numbers.
- <Globe className="size-4.5 inline text-purple-500 mr-2" /> Sender awareness: Recognize known senders and organizations.
- <SlidersHorizontal className="size-4.5 inline text-emerald-500 mr-2" /> Smart rules: Combine different signals instead of relying on a single keyword.
- <ShieldCheck className="size-4.5 inline text-teal-500 mr-2" /> Confidence checks: Avoid presenting uncertain information as fact.

For example, simply seeing the word "payment" shouldn't automatically turn an email into a receipt. Rover should also look at who sent it, what the message says, and whether it contains actual transaction details.

We want useful results, not unnecessary guesses.

Our initial direction is to keep this processing local to the device wherever possible, reducing unnecessary external dependencies and keeping the system lightweight.

Beyond Individual Emails: Rover Intelligence

This is where things become more interesting.

Today, most email applications treat each message as a separate item, even when several messages are related to the same real-world activity.

But people don't think in individual emails.

They think about their orders, payments, meetings, reservations, and conversations.

We want Relay to move closer to that way of thinking.

Imagine ordering something online.

You receive:

- An order confirmation.
- A payment confirmation.
- A shipping notification.
- A delivery update.

Instead of making you search through four separate messages, Rover can recognize that they are connected.

Relay could bring those details together into one delivery timeline.

The same idea can work for other information:

- <Package className="size-4.5 inline text-blue-500 mr-2" /> One delivery timeline: Follow an order from confirmation to arrival.
- <Wallet className="size-4.5 inline text-emerald-500 mr-2" /> One transaction record: Find payment confirmations and related receipts together.
- <CalendarDays className="size-4.5 inline text-purple-500 mr-2" /> One upcoming schedule: See important meetings and appointments.
- <Plane className="size-4.5 inline text-sky-500 mr-2" /> One travel record: Keep booking confirmations and travel updates connected.

We call this direction the Rover Intelligence Index.

It is about connecting information that belongs together, rather than simply collecting more messages.
